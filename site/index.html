MAC DESKTOP · PREVIEW

Mouse, keys and slides.

Control the active Mac app and connected displays from your phone. The remote sends your input; your screen stays on the Mac.

  • Touchpad across every display

    Point across displays, tap, double-tap, scroll or drag. Occasional pointer stepping remains in this beta.

  • Keystrokes into the active app

    Type and use shortcuts in the active Mac window.

  • System media and volume

    Play, pause, skip and adjust volume. Apps decide how they respond to media keys.

  • Switch displays and move windows

    Choose a display to jump the pointer there or move the frontmost window. Turn displays off and wake them while the Mac stays running. Waking a display does not unlock the Mac.

  • Saved shortcuts

    Open saved apps and websites from your phone.

  • Present from your phone

    Start a slideshow in Keynote, PowerPoint or Google Slides on your Mac and keep it active. Use Present on your phone to go forward, go back or end the show.

  • Point and draw on a display

    Choose the audience display and use the phone’s control pad for a laser pointer, pen or highlighter. Marks are temporary and are not saved to slides. The pad does not show the slide. App-window shares may not include the overlay; test it with your display setup first.

  • Reconnect when you need to

    Show the pairing code, manage phones or stop sharing on your Mac.

Setting it up.

Install the Mac helper.

Open the DMG and drag Sofa Remote to Applications. On first launch macOS asks for Accessibility — that is what lets the helper send clicks and keystrokes. Screen Recording is never requested.

Scan the QR with your phone.

Open the menu-bar icon on the Mac and scan the QR with the phone camera. The browser opens the remote. There is no app to install on the phone; the Mac serves a web page. Add it to the Home Screen if you want an icon.

Pair on a network you trust.

Direct LAN uses plain, unencrypted HTTP; use it only on trusted networks. For anything untrusted, point both devices at your own Tailscale network so the connection is encrypted end to end. The pairing code is temporary; a long-lived token is kept on the phone and can be revoked from the Mac.

Before you install
  • Certificate-signed, not Apple-verified or notarised; the first launch may be blocked. Follow the setup guide for the Gatekeeper step on your macOS version — do not disable Gatekeeper.
  • Accessibility permission is required so the helper can send clicks and keystrokes. Screen Recording is not needed.
  • Direct LAN is unencrypted HTTP. Pair only on a trusted network, or route both devices through your own Tailscale network for encrypted transport.
  • The Mac must stay awake and signed in. The remote can sleep or wake the display, but cannot wake a sleeping Mac or unlock one.
  • Expect occasional pointer stepping on busy Wi-Fi and a few browser/provider quirks — see Known Issues.

A few answers.

Does it mirror or record my screen?

No. Sofa Remote sends input and presentation controls to the Mac, and can draw temporary marks on a display. There is no screen capture, recording, streaming or remote-view window.

Do I need an account or a cloud service?

No. There are no accounts, sign-ups, analytics or telemetry. The phone talks to the Mac on the local network, or on your own Tailscale network.

Is it secure?

Direct LAN uses plain HTTP — pair only on a trusted network. For anything else, route both devices through your own Tailscale network for encrypted transport. The pairing code is temporary; a long-lived token is kept on each paired phone, and any phone can be revoked from the Mac.

Can it wake or unlock my Mac?

No. It can sleep or wake the display, but the Mac itself has to be awake and signed in. There is no unlock bypass.

Which phone or browser do I need?

Recent Safari on iPhone has been tested. Chrome on Android may work but has not been tested on a physical device. Older browsers may not pair. There is nothing to install on the phone; adding it to the Home Screen gives it an icon.

Any paid tier, upsell or ads?

No. It's MIT-licensed and free. There is a Ko-fi link if you want to buy a coffee; nothing is gated.
